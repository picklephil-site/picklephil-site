Origin Story

Before there were Avengers, there were guys in a cramped New York office making comics on a deadline.

1939

Timely Comics

Publisher Martin Goodman launches Timely Comics. Its first issue introduces the Human Torch and Namor the Sub-Mariner — pulpy, two-fisted heroes for a Depression-era audience hungry for escapism.

1941

Captain America Punches Hitler

Joe Simon and Jack Kirby's Captain America Comics #1 hits newsstands with Cap decking Adolf Hitler on the cover — months before the U.S. even entered WWII.

1961

Marvel Comics Is Born

Writer-editor Stan Lee, tired of formulaic heroes, teams with artist Jack Kirby on Fantastic Four #1 — flawed, bickering heroes with real personalities. The company rebrands as Marvel Comics.

1962–63

The Golden Run

In a two-year sprint, Lee and his collaborators (Kirby, Steve Ditko, and others) introduce Spider-Man, the Hulk, Thor, Iron Man, and the X-Men — the bedrock of the entire Marvel Universe.

1998–2008

Movies, Then a Gamble

Marvel licenses out characters (X-Men, Spider-Man) to other studios for cash while near-bankrupt. In 2005 it mortgages its own heroes to fund films directly — producer Kevin Feige and Marvel Studios are born.

2008

Iron Man Starts It All

A mid-budget movie about a lesser-known hero, starring a comeback-story Robert Downey Jr., becomes a smash hit — and ends with Nick Fury uttering "Avenger Initiative." The MCU is officially underway.

STAN
LEE

Stan Lee (1922–2018)

Editor, writer, and Marvel's public face for six decades. Alongside artists like Jack Kirby and Steve Ditko, Lee co-created a universe of imperfect, relatable heroes — and made a habit of cameoing in nearly every MCU film, from a security guard in Iron Man to a Watcher in What If...?.

"With great power there must also come great responsibility!"
